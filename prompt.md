# Master Build Prompt: TAMS Infotech Website Update

## 1. Goal and Context

Rebuild and modernise the TAMS Infotech website (https://www.tamsinfotech.com) with an updated brand identity and content.

### 1.1 Primary Objectives

- Establish a recognizable, premium brand identity for TAMS Infotech.
- Communicate its SAP, ERP, cloud transformation, integration, and managed services capabilities.
- Make complex enterprise technology understandable and visually engaging.
- Build credibility with enterprise decision-makers through clear information, genuine proof points, and professional presentation.
- Generate qualified business inquiries and consultation requests.
- Create a consistent experience across desktop, tablet, and mobile.
- Deliver a production-quality implementation with excellent performance, accessibility, responsive behaviour, and maintainable code.

### 1.2 Target Audience

- CIOs, CTOs, and enterprise IT leaders.
- Heads of digital transformation and enterprise architecture.
- ERP and SAP program directors.
- heads of operations, procurement, manufacturing and supply chain.
- Business owners and technology leaders at small, mid-sized, and large enterprises.
- Organisations evaluating SAP implementation, S/4HANA migration, cloud hosting, ERP modernisation, and ongoing application support.

### 1.3 Audience Mindset

These visitors are not looking for technology for its own sake. They need a partner who understands operational complexity, migration risk, business continuity, integration challenges, and long-term maintainability.

The website must communicate innovation without sacrificing trust. It should feel forward-looking and visually ambitious, but never gimmicky, cryptic, or disconnected from actual business needs.

## 4. Art Direction

### 4.1 Storytelling

Imagine opening the website to a dark (blue, not black), cinematic scene. A factory is running. Machines are moving data. Data is flowing between systems. Somewhere, an order is being processed, inventory is being updated, and a shipment is being prepared.

At first, everything appears to work independently.

Then, the camera pulls back, and the visitor begins to see the connections: the factory is linked to its supply chain, the supply chain to its inventory, the inventory to financial systems, and all of them to a shared digital infrastructure.

The connections illuminate a larger system.

**That's the story of TAMS:** helping organisations turn fragmented, complicated technology into connected systems that work together.
The emotional progression is:

- 1. Complexity: Modern businesses run on hundreds of interconnected processes, systems and decisions.
- 2. Connection: TAMS brings system, data, infrastructure and business processes together.
- 3. Clarity: Businesses gain more reliable operations, better visibility, and a foundation for growth.

This concept is grounded in TAMS's enterprise technology focus, but it is a creative direction rather than a claim about the company's own official brand narrative.

### 4.2 Concept

One continuous, luminous industrial-design studio. You fall into it from the preloader and the camera never leaves it again, even between routes. As you scroll, the world assembles itself in the order that chiefs of operations would recognise. The conceptual progression is:

> raw material become parts -> parts become machines -> machines connect into a plant -> the plant connects to SAP

Every chapter is a _place_ in that one world. Routes are rooms in the same building. Changing pages floods the lens with a liquid of brand blue, the camera is carried to the next room underneath it, and the liquid drains away. The physical world gradually becomes a visualisation of enterprise technology.

### 4.4 Typography and Iconography

Use a typography system that combines editorial character with technical precision. Use fluid typography where appropriate, with responsive limits to prevent oversized or awkward text on narrow screens.

**Display typography**

Use a comtemporary, high-quality sans-serif typeface with strong character and excellent readability at large sizes.

Display headlines should feel architectureal: large, controlled, and spatially composed.

Use deliberate line breaks and restrained text widths. Headlines should interact with the visual environment without becoming difficult to read.

**Body typography**

Use a highly legible sans-serif typeface for descriptions, navigation, technical information, and service details.

Body copy must remain comfortably readable on desktop and mobile. Avoid excessively small labels and low-contrast secondary text.

**Iconography**

Use Solar Duotone Boly by 480 Design via Iconify, licensed under CC BY 4.0, as specified in the project brief.

Use icons sparingly and consistently. They should supplement labels and technical diagrams, not replace clear descriptions.

Use simple, consistent line and duotone treatments. Avoid mixing unrelated icon families or using icons merely to fill empty space.

Confirm the selected icon assets and applicable attribution requirements before production.

### 4.5 Motion Language

Motion is part of the storytelling system, not decoration. The site should feel fluid, precise, tactile, and physically coherent.
Use three levels:

1. Narrative motion: camera movement, major transformations, and page transitions.
2. Explanatory motion: data flows, component assembly, and interactive diagrams.
3. Interface motion: hover states, buttons, menus, and form feedback.

The third level must remain quick and unobtrusive.

**Scrolling**

Use Lenis for smooth scrolling where appropriate.

Coordinate scroll behaviour with the animation timeline and ensure compatibility with native browser navigation, focus management, anchor links, and reduced-motion preferences.

Do not force smooth scrolling on every interaction or create long pinned sections that make the website difficult to use.

**Camera movement**

The camera is the primary narrative instrument.

Use slow, deliberate movement to reveal scale, relationships, and depth. Movement should have a clear purpose: approaching an object, revealing a connection, transitioning to another zone, or expanding the visitor's understanding of the system

Avoid constant camera motion and excessive depth-of-field effects.

**Cursor interaction**

On supported desktop devices, use a magnetic, morphing cursor ring that responds to interactive elements.

The cursor can subtly change shape when hovering over buttons, objects, hotspots, or draggable components.

Use a restrained flow-field trail in the post-processing pipeline to introduce subtle visual disturbances around the cursor.

Selected small components may respond to proximity through controlled repulsion or displacement.

These effects must never interfere with text selection, clicking, keyboard interaction, or the legibility of the interface.

Disable or simplify cursor-specific effects on touch devices and when reduced motion is requested.

### 4.6 Sound Design and Music

Sound is an extension of the visual storytelling, giving the industrial world a sense of physical presence and scale. The audio experience should feel cinematic, restrained, and technologically sophisticated—not like a generic corporate presentation.

**Ambient soundscape**

Build a subtle industrial atmosphere using distant machinery, low mechanical hums, soft hydraulic movements, metallic resonance, and the occasional sound of a component locking into place. As individual components connect into a larger system, the soundscape should gradually become more cohesive, reflecting the transition from complexity to connection and clarity.

**Music**

Use a minimal, cinematic electronic score with warm synthesizers, restrained percussion, deep atmospheric textures, and gradual harmonic progression. The music should convey precision, momentum, and confidence without becoming dramatic or overpowering. Avoid aggressive industrial noise, predictable corporate motivational music, and overly futuristic sci-fi sound effects.

**Interactive audio**

Use carefully timed sonic details to reinforce meaningful interactions: a subtle mechanical click when components connect, a soft tonal accent when a system becomes active, or a restrained transition sound when moving between narrative chapters. Every sound should communicate an event or reinforce the physicality of the environment.

**Implementation and accessibility**

- Audio must be muted by default and never autoplay with sound.
- Provide an optional, clearly visible sound toggle.
- Allow ambient effects and music to be controlled independently where practical.
- Keep interface sounds subtle, short, and infrequent.
- Synchronize audio cues with visual events without making either dependent on the other.
- Respect reduced-motion preferences and ensure the experience remains complete without audio.
- Optimize audio assets for fast loading and avoid delaying access to the website.
- Use properly licensed or original audio assets suitable for commercial use.

**The guiding principle:** Sound should make the digital factory feel tangible, not demand the visitor's attention.

## 5. Website Architecture and Navigation

Build a complete multi-age website (with SPA routing) rather than a single long landing page.

The homepage introduces the narrative. Dedicated pages provide the depth necessary for enterprise buyers to evaluate specific services, solutions, products, and evidence.

Use a persistent but unobtrusive navigation system. THe navigation should adapt to the current visual environment without sacrificing readability. Provide clear access to service details and relevant solution pages through well-structured menus.

## 6. Preloader and First-Visit Experience

The preloader is the visitor's entrance into the industrial world.

Create a brief, art-directed loading sequence that feels intentional and directly connected to the environment. Keep the sequence short and proportional to the actual loading process. Do not display a fake percentage or hold the visitor on a preloader after essential content is ready.

Requirements:

- Show meaningful loading progress where it can be measured.
- Provide a static fallback for graphics failures.
- Respect reduced-motion settings.
- Ensure keyboard users can access available content.
- Do not replay a long intro on every route change.
- Make the experience usable if a model or texture or shader fails to load.

## 7. Final Creative Direction

The finished website should feel like an intersection of industrial design, architectural visualization, enterprise technology, and premium digital storytelling.

The environment is tactile and physical. The interactions are precise. The typography is confident. The animations have purpose. The content communicates real business capabilities.

The central visual progression is from individual components to machines, from machines to operational systems, and from operational systems to a connected enterprise.

The visitor should leave understanding not only what TAMS Infotech does, but why its capabilities matter to organizations managing complex enterprise environments.

The guiding principle: Make enterprise complexity visible, make the connections understandable, and make the next step obvious.

Prioritize business credibility, narrative coherence, technical excellence, accessibility, and performance over unnecessary visual spectacle.
